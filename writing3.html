ABSTRACT

Gacha (deriving from the Japanese term, gashapon) is a gaming system that allows players to gamble for characters, weapons, upgrades, or other items through microtransactions (in-game purchases) which is heavily popularized in Asian gaming communities. While these types of games tend to have small, niche audiences, especially in the West, Genshin Impact has broken this tradition by becoming the most popular and most financially successful gacha in a matter of months, even surpassing many triple-a games (large games released by major publishing companies) in terms of users and revenue. This study aims to determine the cause of Genshin Impact’s unparalleled success by diving deep into its gacha system and comparing it to the systems of other popular, yet less successful titles: Fate/Grand Order, Honkai Impact 3rd, Fire Emblem Heroes, Arknights, and AFK Arena. Each of these games' respective gacha systems were analyzed for their maximum amount of payment needed (yet still most cost-effective) to obtain a best ranked character, as well as determining the least amount of characters users can obtain while remaining F2P (free to play). These aspects were then analyzed in order to determine what trends within Genshin Impact’s gacha system prove to be the most profitable.

Keywords: Genshin Impact, miHoYo, HoYoverse, video games, mobile games, game design, gacha, gacha system, loot boxes, in-game transactions, microtransactions, F2P, free to play, freemium


INTRODUCTION AND LIT REVIEW
Shift to Microtransactions

Since the mid-2000’s, paying for video games has been dramatically altered (Zendel, et al., 2020). Initially, video games were bought as a finished product,with one set price, in the form of discs for consoles or coin-op arcade games (Roessel and Švelch, 2021). However, as technology progressed and allowed people to pay for products digitally, microtransactions emerged. Microtransactions allow players to obtain power-ups, cosmetics, characters, weapons, additional content, or other in-game items from directly inside the game to alter the look or feel of their experience (Tirtasamita, 2020). Since their development, microtransactions have exploded in popularity and have quickly become one of the main sources of revenue for developers within the gaming industry, especially within the realm of mobile gaming (Reza, et al., 2018). According to a 2018 study conducted by Superdata, the digital video game industry earned $119.6B, with $35.7B or 30.0% from PC games, and $12.7B or 10.6% from console games, and $61.3B or 51.2% corresponding to purchases from mobile games (Superdata, 2019). Of this 51.2%, the highest earning mobile games were all games that presented themselves as free but featured additional content through the purchase of microtransactions, otherwise known as “freemium” (Camerero, et al., 2021). The abundance of these freemium games eventually became so popular that Apple opted to change the purchase button from “free” to “get” (Brian, 2014).



Genshin Impact's Rise to Fame

Genshin Impact’s parent company, HoYoverse, founded in 2012 by three graduate students from Shanghai Jiaotong University (HoYoverse, 2022) is responsible for the creation of Genshin Impact. They started off as a small indie developer called miHoYo, from 2012 to 2014, and frequently teetered on the edge of bankruptcy (HoYoverse, 2022). Eventually, their team finally found a fit with Honkai 2, their biggest change being shifting to the gacha model. While it didn’t gain much traction in the West, the game easily became a top-10 grossing title in China (HoYoverse, 2022). However, it wasn’t until September 2020 when miHoYo would release Genshin Impact.
Genshin Impact is an anime-inspired action role-playing gacha game released on mobile (iOS and Android), PC, and Playstation. The game takes place in the magical world of Teyvat, home to seven distinct nations tied to different elements and gods. The story follows a character known as the Traveler who travels across Teyvat’s seven nations in search for their lost twin sibling. The game started its development in 2017 and was released for iOS, Android, Playstation 4, and PC on September 28, 2020 (King, 2021). It was later released for Playstation 5 in April 2021 and is currently preparing for its eventual release to the Nintendo Switch and Steam.



The Gacha System

The structure of freemium games widely differs among regions, especially within Western and Eastern markets. Whereas Western games have relied on methods such as lootboxes, the most profitable freemium system for games in the East (specifically Japan) is the utilization of gacha (Lax & Mackenzie, 2019). While they function similarly to microtransactions in the West, gacha systems make it so players have to gamble using random number generators (RNG) for their virtual rewards using real money. They also differ in their rewards. Whereas most Western games offer cosmetic items like skins, dances, and weapons, most gacha games offer characters or weapons that alter aspects of core gameplay (Koeder & Tanaka, 2017). The gacha system soon became a staple of Japanese gaming, and slowly grew more popular throughout Asia, only being popular in the West among very small and niche communities (Lax & Mackenzie, 2019).

The gacha system has its roots in the gashapon model, a type of vending-machine in Japan that would dispense toys for children (Yuen-Ki, 2019). Because of the toys being dispensed on a purely random vending machine, children had to rely on luck to obtain the specific toy they wanted. Modeled after these dispensers, the gacha system requires players to obtain a virtual currency in order to obtain a character or item dispensed at random (Fujihara & Shibuya, 2020). The virtual currency required for these transactions can be obtained in two different ways (Yuen-Ki, 2019). Typically in gacha games, there are many ways to obtain virtual currency for free, whether it be via gameplay, sharing or promoting the game, or participating in events; however, these ways of gaining in-game currency are typically slow. The explosion of Genshin Impact is unlike anything the Gacha Industry has ever seen before. Only six months after its initial release, Genshin Impact generated over $1 billion in just mobile spending. For comparison, it took the mobile game sensation Pokémon Go nine months to reach $1 billion, and 11 months for Clash Royale (Sensor Tower Intelligence, 2020). This easily led it to Genshin Impact becoming the fastest growing title in the App Store, easily becoming the most popular gacha title by a considerable margin. (Sensor Tower Intelligence, 2020). It led global gaming conversations throughout the 2020s, becoming the most talked about game on Twitter of 2021. Genshin Impact managed to edge out global sensation Wordle (King, 2022), and surpass triple-a titles such as Apex Legends, Final Fantasy, Animal Crossing, Minecraft, and Fortnite (Mazanko, 2022). By the end of 2021, the game was estimated to have an audience of over 17 million on mobile devices alone (King, 2021). The game also earned countless awards, such as “iPhone Game of the Year” in the App Store Best of 2020; the 2021 Apple Design Awards for Visuals and Graphics; Google Play’s “Best Game of 2020”; Tap Tap Game Awards 2020’s “Game of the Year”, and was nominated for “Best Mobile Game” and “Best RPG Game” at The Game Awards 2020 (Sahbegovic, 2021). In this paper, I aim to find out what allows Genshin Impact to generate so much more revenue in comparison to its competitors by examining which specific design tactics most affect the likelihood of user spending on microtransactions?


METHODOLOGY

This study aims to determine what aspects of Genshin Impact make purchasing microtransactions most desirable through the lens of Genshin Impact. This study is also heavily based on the model provided in Yuen-Ki’s study, Consumer Motivation in Gacha-Based Mobile Gaming, which defines the gacha system (Figure 1.1). I will use a three-step, mixed method, correlational study, utilizing both quantitative and qualitative analyses to conduct this research and measure user response. The three key steps are in the following order: Character Revenue and Popularity Analysis; Character Cost Comparison; and Analysis of Provided F2P Opportunities.

Additionally, I will take all the data collected from my in-depth analysis of Genshin Impact’s core game design and compare them to those of similar titles in order to properly deduce how this game stands out among other gacha games. For the games I’ve decided to compare it to, I wanted to make sure the gacha titles were deemed successful, with gacha systems and communities similar to those of Genshin Impact. Therefore, I made sure to create a criteria for the games I wanted to look at to make sure my analysis would help me produce the best results possible. This criteria is as follows:


  • Have a gacha system implemented as the main source of getting characters
  • Operate on a mobile platform, or at least have mobile as an option
  • Earned at least $20M within a year

Using this criteria, the gacha titles I’ve decided to analyze for my comparison are: Fate Grand/Order, Honkai Impact 3rd, Fire Emblem Heroes, and Arknights.

My first step, Character Revenue and Popularity Index, will look at each of the featured characters across updates 1.0 to 2.5 and look at how much revenue each of them earned. I will also be observing each of these characters’ Google keyword search volume throughout this time to correlate with their revenue. My second step, Character Cost Comparison, will calculate the required USD spent in Genshin Impact’s gacha system to earn a character and compare this to the costs of other titles. My third and final step, Analysis of Provided F2P Opportunities, will deduce how much worth a player can earn from Genshin Impact and their competitors if they never engage in microtransactions and play them for free.

HYPOTHESIS

Based on my previous research and my own experiences with Genshin Impact, I created a hypothesis that the main contributor to Genshin Impact’s massive success in comparison to other gacha games is its large amounts of F2P currency and lower probability rates in comparison to other gacha games.

One of the main draws toward Genshin Impact is its massive shift in gameplay compared to other gachas. The open-world format that Genshin Impact adopts is most commonly seen in triple-A games, but rarely in mobile platforms (Wen, 2020). This gameplay format allows Genshin Impact to provide variety in earnable F2P currency, by tying them to exploration of new regions, unlocking chests, finding enemies spawn naturally in the world, or by uncovering ancient history. This format also allows players to explore these elements at their own pace. I believe this leads to a large amount of F2P earnable currency for players, allowing HoYoverse to employ the Reciprocation Effect. This is one of the most prominent strategies across these freemium games (Farrell, 2014). This strategy uses the psychological effect of reciprocation, the feeling of wanting to pay back one’s kind deeds with kind deeds of their own, by trying to have their games be as “kind” and “generous” to their players as possible (Nataraj, 2019). Developers achieve this by making their game seem free and depicting themselves as small, down-to-earth, and relatable as they can. This causes the user to subconsciously see the game being sold for free as an “innately friendly and pleasant action” (Farrell, 2014), compelling them to reciprocate with a friendly action of their own, like purchasing microtransactions. I believe that Genshin Impact employs this tactic through their mass amount of F2P-earnable currency and attempts to use this to cover up how much currency is actually needed to obtain a character due to their low base probability and high roll cost, or in other words, utilize the common tactic of price shrouding (Shulman & Geng, 2019).

DISCUSSION

This study initially hypothesized that the biggest contributors to Genshin Impact’s lead in profit would be a result of their large amount of F2P-earnable currency and low base probability. While the data I’ve collected does support this hypothesis, what I’ve discerned to be the most contributing factors diverts quite drastically from my original ideas.



Character Revenue Index

The most popular team role proves to be Main DPS with a total revenue of $151,523,764 and an average of $16,835,973.80 per character. The least popular was DPS/Sub-DPS with a total revenue of $88,699,145 and average of $11,087,393.13 per character. Females proved to be significantly more popular than males, with an average of $15,456,601.78 per female character compared to $13,644,902.36 per male character. The most popular weapon type was polearm with a total revenue of $126,699,280 and an average of $18,095,611.43 per character. The least popular was sword with a total revenue of $47,580,023 and an average of $11,895,005 per character. Finally, the most popular element type in terms of average revenue was electro, earning an average of $19,212,322.30 per character. However, electro was also the element with the least amount of featured 5 star characters. Therefore, I’m also taking into account the most popular element in terms of total revenue, which was pyro with a total revenue of $76,078,964. The least popular element was hydro, with a total of $35,217,477 and an average of $8,804,369.25. According to these trends along the data, we can assume that the highest performing character would be a character who is female, a main DPS, uses either the electro or pyro element, and wields a polearm. Interestingly enough, this description matches two of the best performing characters in the game, Hu Tao, who had the third highest earning banner, and the Raiden Shogun, who had the highest. Even when removing their revenues from the total before calculating the averages, the data still remains the same. Therefore, we can conclude that the best performing characters being female, a main dps, a polearm, with an electro or pyro element.

However, according to the differences in revenue within the data, one of the biggest determinants of a character’s success is being a female main DPS. While the other factors such as weapon type and element type do have an effect on a character’s performance, the differences between them aren’t as drastic as changes in team role or gender. This correlates with much of the prior research done surrounding gacha games in the past. In Lax and Mackenzie’s study of Desire and Monetization in Japanese Mobile Games, they defined a player’s want of a specific character as an “excessive parasocial desire” (Lax & Mackenzie, 2019). Additionally, it’s been reported that 55% of Genshin Impact players are male and the average age is 35 years old (Knezovic, 2021). We can then assume that the popularity of these characters is fueled by a parasocial sexual desire. Video games are often renowned for their aspect of escapism, and gacha games even more so.



Character Cost Comparisons

The data revealed in this section of my study seemed to most closely resemble my initial hypothesis. Out of all of the games listed, Genshin Impact did prove to have the lowest base probability rate of 0.6%. However, the data also showed that they simultaneously managed to have one of the lowest roll requirements for a guarantee at 90 rolls. The significance of these two accomplishments occurring within the same system seems insignificant until plugging Genshin Impact’s base probability into the cumulative probability formula, ignoring guarantees and soft pity. At 90 rolls, Genshin Impact would have a 41.8% chance of receiving a 5 star character, which compared to the other games at their guaranteed number of rolls (excluding F/GO), is the lowest chance given. This means that the numbers presented in Genshin Impact’s gacha system appear to be much more controlled than the other games in comparison. The lower probability given to players before soft pity activates, ensures that they are much more likely to rely on the guarantee, and less likely to receive their wanted character early for less money.



Analysis of Provided F2P Options

It is clear that Genshin Impact didn’t prove to have the most offerable F2P currency out of all the options, and instead ranked 4th most, being behind Arknights, AFK Arena, and Honkai Impact 3rd, respectively. However, when we analyze this data in terms of guaranteed characters earnable through F2P, Genshin Impact jumps from 4th to 2nd. This means that while Genshin Impact may not provide the most F2P currency, the amount of currency it still offers proves to have a significant amount of value. My initial hypothesis of this currency also being earned through open-world gameplay, such as exploration, proved itself to be false. Instead, a majority of this currency is earned through time-based opportunities. This in turn urges users who wish to remain F2P to log-in more frequently in comparison to other gacha titles. This is a common tactic used in many games, and when paired with the added time pressure of limited banners, events, and other in-game items can lead towards a higher rate of addiction among players. This makes them more susceptible to irresponsible spending (Schöber & Stadtman, 2020).


CONCLUSION
Limitations and Future Discussions

Due to the nature of Genshin Impact, there is a possibility that this research could be refuted by the next update and need to be replicated. Genshin Impact is a massive game that is constantly updating, and changes can be made to its system at any point in time. Additionally, Genshin Impact is still in very early stages of production. Their open world currently features only 3 main regions—Mondstadt, Liyue, and Inazuma—yet there are 5 more still in development—Sumeru, Natlan, Fontaine, Sneznyha, and Khaenriah (Fandom Wiki, 2022). Many of the games that Genshin Impact was compared to have already finished their main storylines and are uploading content to their games at either a much slower pace, or more minimally overall.

Another limitation that presented during my research is the niche nature of gacha games, especially in the West. This made it especially difficult to gather data relating to this subject. Many of the papers, theses, and journals I sourced during my research process are based in Eastern countries such as Japan, China, or Korea. As someone who is based in America and only knows how to read and write in English, I had to rely on the limited studies available in English and related studies on similar Western games covering loot boxes and American mobile apps.

Since a lot of research on the gaming community, nonetheless the gacha gaming community is very limited in the Western world, this study can serve as a starting point for many areas of future research. One of the biggest questions I encountered while conducting my literature review was: How do the marketing strategies of these games affect their success? Many gacha games manage to build a majority of their audience through social media advertisements and influencer sponsorships. Yet, a lot of these advertisements and sponsorships follow different terms. Some sponsorships require a video influencer to discuss the game in the beginning of their video, while others may require them to actually play the game or make a full video dedicated to their game. Some games such as AFK Arena even put the influencer they sponsored into the game as their own obtainable character (Gregson-Wood, 2020). Social Media ads tend to be even more varied, with ads ranging from gameplay footage, to scripted commercials, to animated cutscenes (Genshin Impact, n.d.). Therefore, many studies could be conducted analyzing these marketing strategies, and deducing which ones generate the most revenue. Other studies may also be done to address many of the limitations presented in this study, by analyzing the changes in Genshin Impact past the 2.5 update or comparing them to newer games that plan to be released, such as HoYoverse’s future title, Honkai: Star Rail.



Implications

Genshin Impact has swept across the West like no other gacha game, and while this sounds like a commendable achievement on the surface, the ignorance surrounding the gacha genre makes this feat quite dangerous to consumers. As mentioned in Yuen-Ki’s in-depth study surrounding the gacha system, it’s defined as using virtual currency to spend on in-game items with a specific probability of being earned (Figure 1.1). While this currency can be earned through gameplay, this is somewhat limited in comparison to the purchase of them through real-world currency, especially considering the game’s added time pressure through the existence of update patches and character banners. This system shares many qualities with the real world concept of gambling or lottery (Koeder & Tanaka, 2017). However, gacha titles, including Genshin Impact, are not marketed as such. Genshin Impact has seen immense publicity in the past year and a half, as advertising campaigns on YouTube totaled 880 million views (Genshin Impact, n.d.). They’ve also conducted a multitude of sponsorships with YouTubers and social media influencers with thousands of followers (Khan, 2020), earning their own category on Twitch with an average user base of over 12,000 (TwitchTracker, 2022). Furthermore, in the Western world, many government regulations exist surrounding gambling and lottery systems, including age limits, however, gacha games such as Genshin Impact are not regulated as such.

Based on the results presented in this study, it can be confirmed that Genshin Impact places heavy emphasis on consistent daily gameplay. This has already been observed in many gacha games before such as Fate/Grand Order. In 2018, the Wall Street Journal published an article about a Japanese man who spent over $70k while playing (Epstein & Inada, 2018). However, Fate/Grand Order not only garners a lot of their fanbase from Eastern audiences, but the game is also based on the anime franchise, Fate, which has a large majority of its viewers comin from Japan (Statista, 2020). This means that a majority of the players of Fate/Grand Order are already familiar with the anime genre, and most likely gacha games. Genshin Impact differs from this since the story presented in their game is original, meaning that word of mouth can only stem from advertisements, and with advertisements appearing so commonly throughout social media, it’s not guaranteed that everyone would be well-versed with the dangers associated with the genre. Therefore, studies such as this become essential for less-informed consumers, since the only way for games such as Genshin Impact to take advantage of their users is for them to not know any better.
